# Blekhman (2026), "What is biology research for?" Summary

**Citation.** Blekhman R. What is biology research for? *PLOS Biology*, Perspective, published 15 Oct 2026. doi:[10.1371/journal.pbio.3004075](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.3004075). Section of Genetic Medicine, University of Chicago. The piece is about 1,100 words with 10 references, and is open access under PLOS's CC BY licence. Competing interests: "RB is a member of PLOS Biology's editorial board." The full text was read from the publisher's XML on 16 Oct 2026; the PDF is in this folder.

## Argument
1. **The surge.**
   - Submissions escalated from late 2022; arXiv's monthly submissions "nearly doubled", with timing that matches generative AI.
   - arXiv's 2025 CS review/position-paper rule and its 2026 one-year ban for unchecked AI content are "reasonable, but they address symptoms rather than the underlying problem".
2. **Core claim.**
   - "It is now straightforward to generate a plausible scientific paper without doing any actual research."
   - The paper "has come loose from the science it was meant to represent".
3. **The prior question.** Following Terence Tao's essay "Mathematics in the age of AI" (arXiv, 2026, doi:10.48550/arXiv.2608.16753), Blekhman argues the field must first state what biology research is for. He lists five goals:
   - explain living systems, at every scale;
   - improve human health;
   - build resources: data, model organisms, databases, methods, software;
   - train the next generation;
   - keep the trust of the public that pays (the "least frequently named, but arguably the most important" goal).
4. **The proxy.**
   - Papers used to advance some of these goals, so evaluation collapsed quality into counts, venues and citations.
   - The proxy was flawed (he cites Smaldino & McElreath), but "a paper took effort to produce, and that effort served as evidence".
   - "Generative AI has broken this already-flawed proxy completely."
5. **Objection: paper mills existed before.** His answer is that even a marginal paper required labour; AI "eliminates this link between manuscript production and authentic academic endeavor".
6. **Objection: the surge in biology is smaller.**
   - He concedes this is partly true: biology's bottlenecks are experimental, not computational.
   - But computational biology is already affected, and "there are no brakes on the system": careers need papers, publishers earn fees, and AI companies sell tokens.
7. **Prediction.** Without a deliberate response, evaluation falls back on "older, cruder proxies, such as journal prestige and impact factor", reversing DORA/Leiden progress.
8. **Proposal.**
   - Acknowledge that "paper counts are meaningless", abandon the current incentive system, and define evaluation aligned with the field's goals.
   - This is a job for journals, funders and universities alike. Start by asking what biology research is for.

## Relation to our essay
- **Shared ground:**
  - effort as the signal;
  - Goodhart's law;
  - arXiv's measures;
  - the DORA/Leiden context;
  - the incentive treadmill.
- **Where our essay differs or goes further:**
  - **Hollow papers vs real research.** Blekhman's case is the paper produced *without research*. Ours is that even papers reporting real research lose the effort signal, because the effort becomes invisible.
  - **What the field falls back on.** He predicts a fallback to prestige. We document a different fallback already under way: rationing. Its examples are NIH's six-application cap, arXiv's two-a-month cap from 1 Oct 2026 (after his references were accessed), and conference quotas. AI-automated evaluation is the likely next step.
  - **"No brakes."** The caps are brakes, just crude ones; this is a direct point of engagement.
  - **Data.** We have field-level preprint data. Biology (bioRxiv) is +10% year on year in Q3 2026, consistent with his experimental-bottleneck point, while medicine (medRxiv, COVID preprints removed) is +29% and mathematics +78%. We also have the arXiv mathematics prolific-author concentration.
  - **What he does not address:**
    - the external driver (corporate concentration, chat data);
    - the acceleration-vs-exploration test;
    - the documented process record.
  - **His five goals give section 3 a hook.** AI in the ideal world advances the goals, not the paper count.
