# Pre-registration Amendment 1 (ACTIVE): better measures for H1

*Drafted 16 Oct 2026. The author wrote: "H1 requires a bit more work and analysis. I am not sure if it is right but we should find better measures." This amendment adds tests that measure experimental dependence at the level of individual papers, instead of assuming it from archive or category labels. The author approved it in chat on 16 Oct 2026 ("sí, apruebo la enmienda"), and this commit activates it. At activation, no paper-level classification of bioRxiv, medRxiv or arXiv subcategories has been analysed by growth.*

*Note on validation: the human validation must be done by people (the author, plus any colleagues), not by an AI. An AI validating the AI classifier would be circular and would not meet PLOS's requirement that the authors validate AI outputs.*

## What changes, and why

- **The v1 problem.** v1 defined "experimental" by archive or category label (hep-ex vs hep-th; wet-lab vs computational categories). The reviewers showed why that fails:
  - experimental archives contain many papers without new data (Reviewer 3: in a sample of 60 small-team hep-ex records, only 1 reported new measurements);
  - authors choose their own categories;
  - three contrasts give little power.
- **The better measure.** Classify each paper by whether it **reports new empirical data** (ND) or not (NN), as in the active pre-registration (`preregistration_new_data_test.md`). Then use that measure in two new ways:

### Test A — biology and medicine, at paper level (confirmatory)

- **HN-bio (bioRxiv).** Papers that do not report new experimental data grew faster in 2025–2026 than papers that report new experimental data, relative to their pre-2023 trends.
  - NN here means: computational analysis of existing or public data, methods or software, theory or modelling.
  - ND here means: new wet-lab, field or clinical-sample data generated by the authors.
- **HN-med (medRxiv, COVID-19 excluded).** The same hypothesis for medicine.
  - NN: secondary analyses of existing datasets, registries, electronic health records or public data; meta-analyses; modelling.
  - ND: new primary data collection, such as trials, new cohorts or recruitment, surveys, or newly collected clinical samples.
- **Estimand, model, inference and decision rule:** as in §5–§7 of the active pre-registration, with server-specific codebooks (Appendix B).

### Test B — dose–response across categories (confirmatory; the main gain in power)

- **HN-dose.** Across categories, the departure of output from its pre-2023 trend in 2025–2026 is smaller where a larger share of papers reports new data. The share is measured in 2019–2022.
- **Categories:**
  - arXiv subcategories with at least 300 new papers a year;
  - bioRxiv's subject categories;
  - medRxiv's subject categories (COVID-19 excluded).
  - Servers are analysed jointly with server fixed effects, and also separately.
- **Exposure:** the ND share of each category, estimated from a random sample of at least 150 papers per category in 2019–2022, classified with the same codebook.
- **Outcome:** each category's 2025–2026 departure from its own 2017–2022 trend (Poisson PML with seasonality, 2020–21 indicators, and dating by v1 submission where available).
- **Estimand:** the slope of log(observed/expected) on the ND share.
  - **Prediction:** a negative slope.
  - **Inference:** a permutation test that shuffles ND shares across categories within each server (10,000 permutations), plus a bootstrap CI.
- **Decision rule:** supported if the slope is negative with permutation p < 0.05 in the joint model, and negative in at least 2 of the 3 servers.

### Exploratory measures (reported, not confirmatory)

- **HEP, data-normalised output (INSPIRE).** For the large LHC experiments, count analysis papers per year relative to the integrated luminosity available and to time since data-taking. Does output per unit of existing data rise in 2025–26?
- **Journal output.** As a check against preprint-adoption artefacts, count journal articles by field from OpenAlex. Recent months are incomplete because of indexing lag, so treat them with care.

## Validation (one combined sheet, coded blind by the author)

- **Composition:** 400 papers.
  - 200 physics/astronomy, as in the active pre-registration;
  - 100 bioRxiv;
  - 100 medRxiv.
- **Blinding:** each paper has a pseudonymous ID, title and abstract (plus comments for arXiv). Dates, server and model labels are withheld.
- **Rule:** κ ≥ 0.60 in each domain to proceed. If a domain falls short, revise the codebook once on a development sample from the exploratory archives, then re-validate.
- **Optional:** a second coder (a colleague) on 100 papers, to report inter-rater agreement between humans.

## Appendix B. Codebooks for bioRxiv and medRxiv (fixed at activation)

**bioRxiv prompt:**

> Classify this biology preprint from its title and abstract into one class. ND — the authors report NEW experimental or observational data they generated (wet-lab experiments, new sequencing or imaging they performed, field sampling, new clinical or patient samples). NN — no new experimental data: computational analysis of existing or public datasets, methods/software/algorithms, theory or mathematical modelling, simulations, reanalysis. U — cannot tell. If new experimental data are combined with computation, choose ND. Answer only ND, NN or U.

**medRxiv prompt:**

> Classify this medical/health preprint from its title and abstract into one class. ND — the authors collected NEW primary data (a trial, a newly recruited cohort or sample, a new survey, newly collected clinical samples or measurements). NN — no new primary data: secondary analysis of existing datasets, registries, electronic health records, administrative or public data (e.g., NHANES, UK Biobank), systematic reviews or meta-analyses, modelling or simulation. U — cannot tell. Answer only ND, NN or U.

## Deviation 1 (recorded 16 Oct 2026, before any growth-by-class analysis)

**What changed.** The author asked to proceed on the assumption that the classifier is adequate, and to complete the human validation the next day ("supón que está bien y mañana lo valido"). The classification and the confirmatory estimates in this plan will therefore be computed **before** the author's blind validation coding, reversing the order in §4 and §5.

**Safeguards:**
1. The classifier (model, version and verbatim prompt in Appendix A/B) is frozen as registered and will not be changed after results are seen.
2. The validation sheet stays blind (no dates, servers or model labels), so the author's coding cannot be steered by the results.
3. **If κ ≥ 0.60 in a domain,** that domain's results stand as confirmatory, with this deviation disclosed.
4. **If κ < 0.60 in a domain,** that domain's confirmatory results are withdrawn and reported as exploratory only. Any codebook revision made after seeing results will be labelled post hoc.
5. The paper will report this deviation in full.
