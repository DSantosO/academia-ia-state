# The author's critique of v2 (collected for v3)

*These are the author's comments in chat, kept in order. Author comments override reviewer suggestions where they conflict.*

## A1. The opening framing of the abstract is wrong (16–17 Oct 2026)

> "Academic evaluation has long inferred merit partly from what its outputs cost to produce: the weeks behind a grant application, an expert's hours behind a review, the years of reading behind a synthesis"

**The author, translated from Spanish:**

> "The start of the abstract is horrible. When has effort been valued? What has been valued is the result. The point is that before, getting good polish required a lot of work and now it doesn't, and that can make output shoot up. That means form doesn't matter as much, but the result has always been what is valued."

**What changes in v3** (applies to the abstract, the Introduction framework and the Discussion):
- **Do not claim that evaluation valued effort or cost.** Evaluation values *results*.
- **The mechanism:**
  - producing a polished, submittable output (a paper, review or application) used to take substantial skilled work;
  - generative AI makes polish cheap;
  - (1) output volume can rise faster than the underlying work;
  - (2) form becomes a weaker cue of quality.
- **Coordinator's note.** Form did work as a *cue* of quality, not as a value in itself. Before LLMs, writing quality predicted outcomes (Kusumegi et al. 2025 report the reversal). That is compatible with the author's point: what is judged is the result; form was evidence about it, and that evidence is now cheap.

**Proposed new opening (draft for the author):**

> Research evaluation judges results, but producing a polished, submittable result (a paper, a review, a grant application) has always required substantial skilled work, which limited how much could be produced and made polish a reasonable cue of quality. Generative AI makes that polish cheap. If the work needed to reach a submittable form falls, output can rise faster than the research behind it, and publication counts change meaning.
