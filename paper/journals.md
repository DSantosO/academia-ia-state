# Where to submit: journals and outlets for the AI-and-academia position paper

*Checked on journal, publisher and library pages on 15 October 2026. **n.v.** means not verified: the page blocked automated access or did not state the detail. Taylor & Francis, Wiley, MIT Press, Cell Press and science.org pages could not be opened. The evidence, quotes and links are in `research_notes/AI academia essay evidence gaps/4_journal_venues.md`. Assumed profile: sole author, affiliated with EMBL (Barcelona) and UPF, working in the life sciences or computational biology.*

**AI-policy classes** (the deciding criterion, because the paper will openly disclose agent-assisted drafting):
- **(a)** allows AI-generated or AI-drafted text with disclosure.
- **(b)** restricts it, for example to language editing only.
- **(c)** prohibits it.

## Bottom line

Run two tracks with **distinct** texts:

- **Life-science track (≈3,500–4,000 words).** Send a presubmission enquiry to **PLOS Biology** for an Essay. If it is declined, try **EMBO Reports (Science & Society)**, then a **Nature-portfolio Perspective**: Nature Human Behaviour, Nature Computational Science or Nature Machine Intelligence.
- **Scholarly track (full 6,000–8,000 words).** Send it to **Minerva**. If declined, try **Postdigital Science and Education**, then **Research Evaluation** or **Science and Public Policy**.

Every venue on both tracks is class (a), and almost all cost you nothing:
- PLOS magazine content, EMBO Science & Society and Nature Comments or Perspectives carry no fee.
- Springer hybrid journals are covered by EMBL's DEAL agreement or UPF's CSUC agreement.
- Fully-OA journals are covered by EMBL's central APC budget. Confirm article-type eligibility with OSIM.

Make **EMBL** the corresponding affiliation. Preprint on Zenodo or MetaArXiv at submission, and on arXiv cs.CY or SocArXiv only after acceptance. Avoid Issues in Science and Technology, Times Higher Education, MDPI and Frontiers.

## Top 3 (ranked), and the order to submit in

1. **PLOS Biology: Essay** (~3,500–4,000 words; "no strict length limits").
   - **Format:** Essays are "opinionated articles on a topic of interest to scientists and to a broader audience" that may "analyze scientific issues with policy implications". They are refereed.
   - **Cost:** no publication fee for magazine articles. EMBL also has a PLOS flat-fee deal.
   - **AI policy (a):** "AI tools may be used to support development or revision of a PLOS submission", with disclosure of the tools, what they changed and how outputs were validated.
   - **Precedent:** it has recently published pieces on LLMs and scientific writing, by Bietti et al. (2026) and Amano et al. (2025). Their article type was not checked.
   - **Reach:** this is where your own community will read it.
   - **Route:** send a presubmission enquiry to biology_editors@plos.org.
   - **Trade-off:** you must condense the argument, probably to §1, §2 and §4, with §3 as the payoff.
2. **Minerva** (Springer): the full paper.
   - **Fit:** its remit is "ideas, traditions, cultures and institutions in science, higher education and research".
   - **Format:** articles of 8,000–10,000 words *including* references; double-blind review; JIF 2.9; Scopus Q1.
   - **AI policy (a):** LLM use is "documented in the Methods section (... or ... a suitable alternative part)".
   - **Cost:** hybrid OA is free through EMBL DEAL (eligibility limited to "original research, peer-reviewed papers, and reviews", so confirm with EMBL's Open Science office, OSIM). Alternatively, use UPF's CSUC Springer quota, if Minerva is on the CSUC journal list.
   - **Speed:** the median first decision is 9 days.
3. **EMBO Reports: Science & Society** (≤3,500 words, ≤15 references). The fallback for item 1.
   - **Format:** it covers "the wider impact of biological research, including policy, communication, education". Editors review it, with peer review at their discretion. Presubmission enquiries go through eJP only, not email.
   - **Cost:** no APC for this article type.
   - **AI policy:** presumed (a). It sits on the Springer Nature platform, and no EMBO-specific AI text was found.

**Order:**
- Life-science track: PLOS Biology enquiry, then EMBO Reports, then an NHB, Nature Computational Science or Nature Machine Intelligence Perspective.
- Scholarly track: Minerva, then Postdigital Science and Education, then Research Evaluation or SPP.

The tracks may run at the same time only if the texts are genuinely different, for example a ~4,000-word essay for biologists and a 7,000-word scholarly argument. Tell each editor about the related manuscript.

**About Postdigital Science and Education** (the scholarly fallback):
- It is the best fit for all five sections, and it has published reflexive human–ChatGPT writing.
- It also published Manabu (2026), *"The Machine Consumes Itself: AI and the End of Publish-or-Perish"*, which is close to your thesis. Cite it and differentiate from it.
- Its journal page shows no impact factor.

**Down-ranked:**
- AI & Society is class (b): "LLM use for tasks other than grammar and translation ... strongly discouraged".
- Issues and THE are class (c).

## Avoid

- **MDPI and Frontiers.** This is a credibility risk for a critique of volume-driven, APC-funded publishing. Clarivate delisted two MDPI titles from Web of Science in 2023. EMBL's 30% MDPI and 10% Frontiers discounts do not change this advice.
- **Class (c) outlets for AI-drafted text:** Issues, THE, and SocArXiv before peer review.
- **Submitting the same text in parallel.** The two tracks must be genuinely distinct, and editors must be told about related manuscripts.

## AI disclosure: what to declare, and how to make it part of the argument

**Strategy.** The thesis is that academia cannot yet tell good AI use from bad. So disclose everything, and present the workflow as the worked example:
- the author sets the thesis, structure and judgements;
- agents search, check and draft;
- the author verifies and rewrites.

Every class (a) venue above permits this *if the disclosure is specific*. Springer Nature explicitly forbids presenting AI-generated "hypotheses, analyses or conclusions ... as human-derived" and "Using an LLM to generate core research reasoning without disclosure". Two habits strengthen the disclosure:
- **Archive the log:** agent instructions, research notes and reference-verification records, on Zenodo or OSF, cited by DOI.
- **Use existing frameworks:** Cwik (2026, *RIPR*) separates "disclosure" from "documentation". The GAIDeT taxonomy (Suchikova et al. 2025, *Accountability in Research*) classifies tasks delegated to AI.

**Where each publisher wants the statement:**
- **PLOS:** in Methods or Acknowledgements, naming the tools, what they affected and *how outputs were validated*.
- **Springer Nature and EMBO Press (Minerva, PSE, HSSC, RIPR, Nature journals, EMBO Reports):** in Methods "or a suitable alternative part". For an essay, use a titled section before the references.
- **OUP:** in the cover letter **and** in the Acknowledgements.
- **SAGE:** at submission. Stress human authorship.
- **Taylor & Francis:** the tool name *with version*, how it was used and why.
- **Wiley:** whether AI "influenced key arguments or conclusions" and how you verified its output.
- **Elsevier/Cell:** a separate "Declaration of generative AI and AI-assisted technologies in the manuscript preparation process".

**Suggested statement.** Fill in the brackets and keep it in the main text.

> **Statement on the use of generative AI.** This essay was written with substantial, deliberate help from AI agents. Its argument partly depends on that use (see Section [4]), so the process is set out here in full.
>
> [Author] conceived the thesis, designed the five-part structure, wrote the research questions given to the agents, made all interpretive and normative judgements, and revised every section line by line.
>
> AI agents ([tool and model name, version; dates of use]) carried out three tasks: (i) searching and screening the scholarly and policy literature; (ii) checking bibliographic records against Crossref, DataCite and arXiv, and checking claims against their sources; and (iii) writing first drafts of sections from the author's outline.
>
> The AI-generated drafts shaped the wording and the selection and order of evidence. They did not set the thesis, the conclusions or the recommendations.
>
> The author checked every reference and quotation against the original source and removed any AI-generated statement that could not be verified. No AI system is an author, and the author takes full responsibility for the content. Agent instructions, research notes and verification records are archived at [Zenodo/OSF DOI].

**Suggested contribution statement (CRediT).** CRediT applies to human contributors only, so the AI's role belongs in the statement above.

> **[Author name]** (EMBL Barcelona; Universitat Pompeu Fabra):
> - Conceptualization.
> - Methodology (design of the AI-assisted literature and drafting workflow).
> - Investigation (direction and quality control of the AI-assisted literature search).
> - Validation (verification of all sources, quotations and claims).
> - Writing – original draft (thesis, structure and key arguments; revision of AI-generated first drafts).
> - Writing – review & editing.
> - Project administration.
>
> *AI tools are not contributors under CRediT; their role is described in the statement on the use of generative AI.*
