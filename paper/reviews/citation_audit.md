### The most serious problems, in order

1. **The Kusumegi output gain is stated as fact, but its critique is now peer-reviewed.** Renault, Bergeaud & Bosquet was published in *PNAS* 123(33):e2618638123 on 11 Aug 2026 and concludes that "first-detection timing alone creates spurious evidence of productivity gains from LLM adoption". The draft still cites it as a preprint and uses the 23.7–89.3% gain as evidence in ¶2.2, ¶2.6b and (indirectly) Box 2. See R06, R07, R09, R17, R74.
2. **Huang et al. (2026) is misreported where the essay claims its own contribution.** The Comment makes three recommendations, not two. The third, "Reward innovation, not just output", proposes measuring "the conceptual distance between a researcher's current and previous work and measures of lasting intellectual disruption, rather than short-term citation accumulation". The second calls for "exploratory grants that have extended evaluation horizons". The essay's own Box 2 measure (distance from a scientist's earlier work) and ¶6.1's fourth proposal (long-horizon funding to reward exploration) therefore overlap with Huang et al. and need crediting. ¶4b.2 says "the remedies of theirs that I have read" (R38).
3. **Hao et al. is overstated where the rival hypothesis is set up.** The source says data availability "appears to be a major impacting factor", not "the main driver" (¶4b.2), and it never measured incentives, so Box 2's "data availability rather than incentives best predicts where AI concentrates" is unsupported (R34, R73). Both rest on supplementary figures of the arXiv v4 author version.
4. **Quotations misattributed or altered.** (a) ¶2.6b quotes "a relatively small proportion of authors are submitting a large number of low-quality papers" as "in arXiv's words"; on arXiv's 1 Oct 2026 post it is a block quotation from Thomas Dietterich. (b) ¶4c.2 attributes "double standard" to Aylin Yener; in Singh Chawla (2026a) "perceived double standard" is the journalist's phrase and Yener's quotation is a question. (c) ¶4c.1 quotes the European Commission as "broad and increasing"; the source says "broad and growing". (d) ¶3.3 paraphrases Asai et al. as saying full automation "is not yet possible"; they write that they "do not claim that LM-based systems can fully automate scientific literature synthesis". (e) The arXiv one-year ban is cited to TechCrunch as something arXiv "reportedly began"; the primary source is an X thread by Dietterich (14 May 2026) announcing it.
5. **Table 1 and ¶4c.2 misdescribe several rationing rules, although primary sources exist for all of them.** ICLR 2027 was announced on 28 Jul 2026 (not "Oct 2026"). AISTATS 2027 does state a reason (generative AI; "Peer review is the scarcest resource of a conference"). The ACL row is an ACL Rolling Review policy for cycles from October 2026, with a lottery for submissions that bring no reviewer. TMLR's quota rule is public. The Table note "All conference rows rest on one news article" is no longer true (R40–R41, R78–R83).

Further problems (details in the table): seven sources labelled "preprint" are now published or accepted (Si et al. 2024 → ICLR 2025, so ¶4a.2's "all are preprints" is false; Latona et al. → *PACM HCI* 2025; Geng & Trotta → Findings of ACL 2025; Apertus → ACL 2026; Bianchi et al. → *Nature Biotechnology*; Elazar & Antoniak → accepted at Findings of EMNLP 2026; Renault et al. → *PNAS*). Tracker statements need fixes (the Epoch lead "changed hands" twice, not three times, in September; the 27B Qwen model was released in August, not October; ASPI documents one flagship going proprietary, not "several"; the "33 to 47 times" compute gap is the note's own calculation from speculative estimates). The CoARA signatory count is out of date (994 listed). ¶5.2 cites the Irish DPC for X, but that source concerns public posts, not chats. Figure 2's claim that Anthropic was "the last major US developer to commit to the contrary" conflicts with the Apple counter-example in the same caption. Reference-list defects include a wrong DOI (Korinek & Vipra), two missing authors (Castellacci et al.), a wrong source type (Singh Chawla 2026a is *Nature Index* news), and a Garante URL that points to a press release, not the provision.
