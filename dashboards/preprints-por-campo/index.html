      How to read these data

      • What is counted. New preprints per quarter. On arXiv, new submissions by primary archive, without replacements or cross-lists. On bioRxiv and medRxiv, new papers without revisions. On PsyArXiv, SocArXiv and EdArXiv, first versions only.
      • Medicine excludes COVID-19. A medRxiv preprint is left out when its title or abstract mentions COVID-19, SARS-CoV-2 or 2019-nCoV. The COVID-19 column in the table shows what was removed. Medicine's 2022 base is low because COVID-19 research crowded out other medRxiv preprints in 2020–2022, which inflates its growth against 2022.
      • A preprint is not a publication. Preprint use differs widely by field: nearly universal in physics and mathematics, partial in biology and medicine, and a minority practice in the social sciences, where psychology dominates. Compare each field's trend, not volumes across fields.
      • Social sciences is a small sample. SSRN and RePEc, the largest economics and social-science repositories, are missing because reliable quarterly counts could not be obtained.
      • Known breaks. arXiv changed its rules between October 2025 and February 2026 (review papers in computer science, endorsement of new authors, a full English version). September 2026 is its busiest month on record; a limit of two submissions a month per person was announced and applied on 1 October 2026. SocArXiv began requiring ORCID and moved humanities papers to another repository in November 2025. Since early 2025 OSF lists every version as a separate record; only first versions count here.
      • Model launches. Claude Opus 4.6 (5 February 2026) and Claude Opus 5 (24 July 2026) are marked as Anthropic milestones. Other labs released models in the same months, so the markers show timing, not cause.
      • arXiv totals. Some arXiv categories are aliases shared by two archives (for example, mathematical physics), so a small share of papers counts in two fields.
