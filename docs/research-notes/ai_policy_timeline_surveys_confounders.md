# AI policy timeline, researcher AI-use surveys, and confounding shocks for academic publishing, peer review and research funding (2019 – 15 Oct 2026)

Verification tags used below: **[P]** = checked on the primary source (official page, notice, blog or raw dataset) during this research; **[N]** = checked in a fetched secondary source (news report, Wikipedia, press coverage); **[S]** = taken only from search-result summaries or snippets (page not opened), so treat as unverified. "Series" names the data series whose level, trend or composition the event could plausibly break. Dates are calendar dates as published (time zones can shift by ±1 day). The web-search budget ran out partway through. Items that could not be checked afterwards are listed under Gaps.

## 1. Treatment-onset dates: generative-AI model and tool releases, and "AI scientist" milestones

### Takeaway
ChatGPT's launch on 30 Nov 2022 is the canonical first treatment date. After it come several plausible secondary onsets: GPT-4 and Claude 1 (Mar 2023); the reasoning and "deep research" agents (Sep 2024 to Feb 2025: o1, DeepSeek-R1, OpenAI deep research, Google AI co-scientist); GPT-5, Gemini 3 and Claude Opus 4.5 (Aug to Nov 2025); OpenAI's free AI LaTeX workspace Prism (27 Jan 2026); and a dense run of 2026 frontier releases. Fully AI-generated research first passed peer review at a workshop in Mar 2025. The first AI-authored and AI-reviewed conference, Agents4Science, was held on 22 Oct 2025.

#### "AI scientist" and AI-review milestones
- **2025-02-19**: Google Research announced the "AI co-scientist", a multi-agent system built on Gemini 2.0 that generates hypotheses and research proposals, offered through a Trusted Tester Program. [P] — [Google Research blog](https://research.google/blog/accelerating-scientific-breakthroughs-with-an-ai-co-scientist/)
- **2025-03-03**: Autoscience Institute claimed that its agent "Carl" had papers accepted through peer review at ICLR workshops. This is reported only in an editor's note. [S] — [R&D World](https://www.rdworldonline.com/sakana-ai-claims-first-fully-ai-generated-paper-passes-peer-review-at-top-ai-conference/)
- **2025-03-12**: Sakana AI announced that a paper fully generated by The AI Scientist-v2 passed peer review at the ICLR 2025 "I Can't Believe It's Not Better" workshop.
  - Scores were 6, 7 and 6 (mean 6.33). No meta-review was performed, and the paper was withdrawn under a pre-agreed protocol.
  - A technical report and open-source release followed on 7 Apr 2025.
  - [P] — [Sakana AI](https://sakana.ai/ai-scientist-first-publication/)
- **Later**: The AI Scientist-v2 was reportedly written up in *Nature* ("Towards end-to-end automation of AI research"); the date was not verified. [S] — [AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/sakana-ai-16335)
- **2025-10-22**: Agents4Science 2025 (Stanford) required AI as primary author and used AI reviewers.
  - It received 315 submissions; 62 incomplete ones were desk-rejected.
  - The remaining 253 were scored by three LLM reviewers, and the top 79 also received a human expert review.
  - 48 papers were accepted, and over 1,800 people registered to attend.
  - [S] — [arXiv 2511.15534](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.15534); [submissions page](https://agents4science.stanford.edu/submissions.html)
- AI-assisted review deployments at AI conferences are listed in section 2: ICLR 2025 feedback agent, AAAI-26 AI review pilot, and Google's Paper Assistant Tool at ICML 2026 and ICLR 2027.

### Inferences
- For ITS designs, a single break at Dec 2022 is too coarse. Plausible step or slope changes cluster at:
  - Dec 2022 to Mar 2023 (ChatGPT, GPT-4, Claude 1);
  - Sep 2024 to Feb 2025 (reasoning models, DeepSeek-R1, deep-research agents);
  - Aug to Nov 2025 (GPT-5, Claude 4.5 family, Gemini 3);
  - late Jan 2026 (Prism, a writing tool aimed directly at papers).
- These onsets are close together and overlap with policy changes and confounders (sections 2–4), so analysts should use event windows and placebo dates rather than a single interruption.
- The Nov 2025 to Feb 2026 window is especially crowded:
  - Gemini 3, Opus 4.5, GPT-5.2, Prism and Opus 4.6;
  - arXiv's English-language and endorsement rules;
  - the US shutdown (Oct–Nov 2025) and NIH rescheduled deadlines.

## 2. Platform, publisher, journal and conference policy events

### Takeaway
Policy reactions accelerated sharply from late 2025, which mechanically breaks submission series:
- **arXiv:** CS review/position-paper restriction (31 Oct 2025), English-version requirement (11 Feb 2026), tightened endorsement (10 Dec 2025 for maths; 21 Jan 2026 for all sections), a one-year ban for unchecked LLM output (May 2026) and a cap of 2 submissions per submitter per month (1 Oct 2026).
- **AI conferences:** per-author caps (CVPR 2025 onward, ICLR 2027, AISTATS 2027, ACL), reciprocal-review obligations with desk-rejection penalties (ICLR 2026, ICML 2026) and AI-review pilots (ICLR 2025, AAAI-26).
- **Journals:** some now cap submissions (JPART from 1 Jul 2026; TMLR from Jun 2026).

Publisher authorship policies (Jan–May 2023) mainly change disclosure behaviour rather than volume.

### Cited Findings
#### arXiv
- **Since 2024**: arXiv has limited each submitter to three active (unannounced) submissions at any time; this predates the 2026 rate limit. **Series:** arXiv submissions from high-volume submitters. [P] — [arXiv blog, 1 Oct 2026](https://blog.arxiv.org/2026/10/01/updated-rate-limit-policy/)
- **2025-10-31**: CS review/survey articles and position papers must already be accepted at a peer-reviewed journal or conference, with journal reference/DOI.
  - Workshop review does not count, and societal-impact papers (e.g., cs.CY) are exempt.
  - arXiv describes this as a change in moderation practice already in force, with no separate effective date.
  - Rationale: arXiv receives "hundreds of review articles every month", most "little more than annotated bibliographies", and generative AI makes them "fast and easy to write".
  - **Series:** arXiv cs submissions and acceptances, especially surveys and position papers; cs rejection rates.
  - [P] — [arXiv blog](https://blog.arxiv.org/2025/10/31/attention-authors-updated-practice-for-review-articles-and-position-papers-in-arxiv-cs-category/)
- **2025-11-21 (announced) → 2026-02-11 (effective)**: All submissions must include a full English-language version, either as the original or as an included translation. **Series:** arXiv submissions by non-English-language authors; this is a non-AI shock to arXiv totals. [P] — [arXiv blog 2025 index](https://blog.arxiv.org/2025)
- **2025-12-10 (maths) → 2026-01-21 (all sections)**: An institutional email address no longer suffices on its own for endorsing new authors; the stated aim is to stem "low-quality, non-scientific submissions". **Series:** first-time submitter counts and new-author share. [P] — [arXiv maths endorsement post](https://blog.arxiv.org/arxiv/2025/12/10/updated-endorsement-policy-for-arxiv-mathematics); [arXiv 2025 index](https://blog.arxiv.org/2025)
- **2026-05 (reported 15 May 2026)**: arXiv CS section chair Thomas Dietterich announced on X that "incontrovertible evidence" of unchecked LLM output triggers a 1-year ban. Examples include hallucinated references and leftover LLM meta-comments. After the ban, the author's papers must first be accepted at a peer-reviewed venue. No official arXiv policy page was found. [N] — [404 Media](https://www.404media.co/new-arxiv-rules-ai-generated-papers-ban/)
- **2026-07-01**: arXiv spun out of Cornell as an independent nonprofit. On 9 Jul 2026 it reported hosting over 3 million articles. **Series:** governance change only, but it may coincide with moderation-capacity changes. [P] — [arXiv blog 2026 index](https://blog.arxiv.org/2026)
- **2026-10-01**: New rate limit for every submitter and category: at most 2 submissions per calendar month and 3 active at any time. Rejected submissions count; the limit applies to the submitter, not co-authors.
  - Volume statistics given:
    - September submissions: 9,869 in 2016, 20,569 in 2024 and a record 40,363 in 2026.
    - The Sep 2026 volume generated almost 9,000 support tickets.
    - Submissions doubled in two years, and cs.AI grew more than 6-fold over the same period.
  - **Series:** arXiv monthly submissions from Oct 2026 (expected level drop, plus possible shifting of submissions onto co-authors).
  - [P] — [arXiv blog](https://blog.arxiv.org/2026/10/01/updated-rate-limit-policy/)
  - *Nature* adds: about 40,000 uploads in Sep 2026 versus about 30,000 a month in Mar–Jul 2026; about 300 volunteer moderators checking about 1,000 manuscripts a day; nearly 4,000 submissions waiting. It also reports that SocArXiv now restricts submissions to one per author per week (start date not given). [N] — [Nature, 15 Oct 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-03194-w)
  - THE adds a quote from Dietterich: "some authors are submitting dozens of papers". [N] — [THE, 2 Oct 2026](https://timeshighereducation.com/news/arxivs-new-preprint-submission-cap-divides-opinion)

#### bioRxiv / medRxiv (openRxiv)
- **2025-03-11**: openRxiv launched as an independent nonprofit overseeing bioRxiv and medRxiv, previously run by Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory. **Series:** governance change; no direct volume rule. [S] — [BMJ Group release](https://bmjgroup.com/openrxiv-launch-to-sustain-and-expand-preprint-sharing-in-life-and-health-sciences/); [Infodocket](https://www.infodocket.com/2025/03/11/openrxiv-launch-to-sustain-and-expand-preprint-sharing-in-life-and-health-sciences-usa/)
- **2025-11-04**: bioRxiv began its first AI-review pilot with q.e.d Science. Authors can opt to send their preprint to the external service, which uses generative AI to analyse claims and evidence and identify "gaps". [S] — [bioRxiv News & Notes](https://connect.biorxiv.org/news/2025/11/04/qed_review_tool)
- **2025-10-13**: arXiv joined openRxiv in a response to an NIH Request for Information (blog title only). [P] — [arXiv blog 2025 index](https://blog.arxiv.org/2025)

#### Publishers and editorial bodies (mainly disclosure and authorship rules)
- **2023-01-24**: Nature/Springer Nature published an editorial with two ground rules: LLMs cannot be credited as authors, and their use must be documented, for example in the Methods section. [P] — [Nature editorial](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-023-00191-1)
- **2023-01-26**: *Science* (editor Holden Thorp, "ChatGPT is fun, but not an author") barred AI-generated text and figures from papers and AI authorship, and treats violations as misconduct. [S] — [GIGAZINE summary](https://wbgsv0a.gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20230127-science-journals-editorial-policies-chatgpt)
- **2023-02-13**: COPE position statement:
  - AI tools cannot be authors.
  - Authors must disclose AI use in writing, images or data analysis, in the Methods section.
  - Authors are fully responsible for AI-generated content.
  - [S] — [COPE](https://publicationethics.org/guidance/cope-position/authorship-and-ai-tools)
- **ICMJE recommendations**:
  - **May 2023**: AI disclosure (cover letter and manuscript) and no AI authorship.
  - **Jan 2024**: AI in peer review; uploading manuscripts may breach confidentiality.
  - **Apr 2025**: update.
  - **Jan 2026**: new standalone Section V, "Use of Artificial Intelligence in Publishing", covering authors, reviewers and editors.
  - [S] — [ICMJE Jan 2024 note](https://icmje.acponline.org/news-and-editorials/updated_recommendations_jan2024.html); [CASRAI on Jan 2026 (S, low-reliability site)](https://www.casrai.org/news/icmje-january-2026-recommendations-update)
- **Elsevier** (original date not verified): AI may be used in writing only to improve language and readability, not to generate scientific insights, and must be disclosed. [S] — [Elsevier policy](https://www.elsevier.com/about/policies-and-standards/the-use-of-generative-ai-and-ai-assisted-technologies-in-writing-for-elsevier)
- **2025-03-13**: Wiley released AI guidelines for authors, citing the roughly 70% of researchers in its ExplanAItions study who want publisher guidance. [S] — [Wiley press release](https://newsroom.wiley.com/press-releases/press-release-details/2025/Wiley-Releases-AI-Guidelines-for-Authors/default.aspx)

#### Journals capping or pausing submissions, and formulaic "floods"
- **2024–2025, formulaic-paper floods** (relevant to desk-rejection and retraction series):
  - Mendelian randomization: BMC Medicine (Sep 2024) urged editors to desk-reject weak MR papers. [S] — [Burgess et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11389083)
  - NHANES: *PLOS Biology* (May 2025) documented 190 formulaic NHANES single-factor papers in 2024 (to early Oct), versus about 4 a year in 2014–2021, and linked the rise to AI-assisted writing and paper mills. [S] — [Suchak et al., PLOS Biol](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12061153/)
- **2026-06**: *Transactions on Machine Learning Research* (TMLR) introduced annual author submission quotas after submissions tripled over the past year, including a 13-fold rise in single-author submissions. [N] — [Nature Index, 13 Oct 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-03153-5)
- **2026-07-01**: *JPART* (Journal of Public Administration Research and Theory) began a pilot cap.
  - Each author may make at most 3 submissions per calendar year, of which at most 2 may be desk-rejected; the count resets on 1 Jan 2027.
  - Submissions were about 45–50 a month in autumn 2023 and 119 in April 2026. The journal attributes the rise "in large part" to generative AI.
  - **Series:** a template for journal-level ITS with an explicit cap date.
  - [P] — [JPART cap policy](https://www.gopmra.org/assets/Secretariat/Policies/jpart-submission-cap-policy.pdf)
- **2026 (various)**:
  - *Philosophy & Public Affairs* bans substantially AI-authored submissions (date not verified). [S] — [Digg summary](https://digg.com/tech/u6w4wvec)
  - *[in education]* closed to new submissions until 10 Aug 2026 and adopted a GenAI policy. [S] — [journal announcement](https://journals.uregina.ca/ineducation/announcement/view/33)
  - A Chronicle of Higher Education piece (Aug 2026) reports editors seeing submission rates "doubled or even tripled" in a year. [S] — [Northeastern repost](https://cssh.northeastern.edu/ai-is-ruining-scholarship/)

#### AI and ML conferences
- **~Jan 2023, ICML 2023**: Papers containing LLM-generated text were prohibited unless the text was part of the experimental analysis. "Light editing" of authors' own text was allowed, and no automated screening was planned. [P] — [ICML 2023 LLM policy](https://icml.cc/Conferences/2023/llm-policy)
- **IJCAI per-author caps**:
  - IJCAI-ECAI-18: 10 submissions per author (main track).
  - IJCAI-21 and IJCAI 2023: 8 per author, with over-limit authors' papers liable to rejection without review.
  - IJCAI 2025: a main-track cap (number not found) and one survey-track paper per author.
  - [S] — [IJCAI-21 CfP](https://ijcai-21.org/cfp/); [IJCAI-23 CfP](https://ijcai-23.org/call-for-papers/); [IJCAI-25 survey CfP](https://2025.ijcai.org/?p=1117)
- **2024-10-09, ICLR 2025 review-feedback agent**: An LLM agent gave optional feedback on a randomly selected subset of reviews within hours of submission; it did not write reviews. [P] — [ICLR blog](https://blog.iclr.cc/2024/10/09/iclr2025-assisting-reviewers/)
  - Results: more than 20,000 reviews received feedback; 27% of those reviewers updated their reviews; over 12,000 suggestions were incorporated; updated reviews grew by about 80 words. **Series:** ICLR review length and quality. [P] — [arXiv 2504.09737](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09737)
- **CVPR 2025**: Each author is capped at 25 submissions; papers beyond the 25th are desk-rejected, and the CVPR opening remarks reported "full compliance". ICCV 2025 used the same cap. **Series:** CVPR/ICCV submissions per author. [S] — [CVPR 2025 changes](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/Conferences/2025/CVPRChanges); [Voxel51](https://voxel51.com/blog/opening-remarks-from-cvpr-2025); [ICCV 2025 changes](https://iccv.thecvf.com/Conferences/2025/Changes)
- **CVPR 2026 (deadlines 6 and 13 Nov 2025)**:
  - The 25-paper cap remains.
  - "Papers containing citations of non-existent material will be rejected when found."
  - Prompt injection is prohibited.
  - Authors are added to the reviewer pool by default.
  - [P] — [CVPR 2026 author guidelines](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/Conferences/2026/AuthorGuidelines)
- **NeurIPS 2025 LLM policy**:
  - Authors may use any tool; LLM use that is part of the method must be described; authors are responsible for all content, including references.
  - Reviewers may not share submissions with any LLM.
  - [P] — [NeurIPS 2025 LLM policy](https://neurips.cc/Conferences/2025/LLM)
  - NeurIPS 2025 had 21,575 valid main-track submissions and 5,290 accepted (24.52%), up from 9,467 submissions in 2020. [S] — [NeurIPS chairs' blog](https://blog.neurips.cc/2025/09/30/reflections-on-the-2025-review-process-from-the-program-committee-chairs/); [Fact sheet](https://media.neurips.cc/Conferences/NeurIPS2025/press/NeurIPS2025-Fact_Sheet.pdf)
  - **Jan 2026**: GPTZero reported at least 100 hallucinated citations in 51 accepted NeurIPS 2025 papers (Fortune: at least 53 papers). [S] — [GPTZero](https://gptzero.me/news/neurips/); [Fortune](https://www.fortune.com/2026/01/21/neurips-ai-conferences-research-papers-hallucinations)
- **2025-08-26, ICLR 2026 LLM policy**:
  - Any LLM use must be disclosed by authors and reviewers.
  - Sharing confidential material with LLMs can lead to desk rejection of the reviewer's own papers.
  - Hidden prompt injections count as "collusion".
  - [P] — [ICLR blog](https://blog.iclr.cc/2025/08/26/policies-on-large-language-model-usage-at-iclr-2026/)
- **ICLR 2026 reciprocal reviewing**:
  - Authors on 3 or more submissions must review at least 6 papers; those who fail to finish by the rebuttal stage risk desk rejection of their own papers.
  - Each paper must register a qualified author to review at least 3 papers.
  - [S] — [ICLR 2026 Author Guide](https://iclr.cc/Conferences/2026/AuthorGuide)
- **2025-11-19, ICLR 2026 response to LLM-generated papers and reviews**: Papers with extensive undisclosed LLM use or hallucinated content are desk-rejected, but only where an AC/SAC finds "concrete evidence". Reviewers who posted LLM-generated low-quality reviews face consequences, including desk rejection of their own papers. [P] — [ICLR blog](https://blog.iclr.cc/?p=1055)
  - Pangram Labs classified about 21% of about 75,800 ICLR 2026 reviews as fully AI-generated. This is a vendor estimate. [S] — [Plagiarism Today](https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2025/12/04/ai-conference-overrun-with-ai-peer-review/)
- **2025-11-27 / 2025-12-03, OpenReview security incident**:
  - A bug, fixed on 27 Nov, leaked reviewer, author and AC identities, and data on over 10,000 ICLR papers (45%) were scraped.
  - ICLR reverted scores to their pre-discussion state, froze discussions, reassigned every paper to a new AC and desk-rejected colluders.
  - **Series:** this structurally breaks ICLR 2026 review and score data.
  - [P] — [ICLR blog Dec 2025](https://blog.iclr.cc/2025/12/)
- **2026-03-31, ICLR 2026 retrospective**:
  - 19,525 valid submissions; 779 desk rejections; 5,042 withdrawals.
  - 5,355 accepted (27.4%); 76,139 reviews by 18,054 reviewers.
  - Papers with confirmed hallucinated references were desk-rejected, which "partially explains" the higher desk-rejection rate.
  - Two LLM detectors were run on all reviews, and flagged reviews were sent to ACs.
  - [P] — [ICLR blog Mar 2026](https://blog.iclr.cc/2026/03/)
  - ICLR 2025 had about 11,603 submissions, so the 2026 growth is about 68%. [S] — [AI Weekly summary](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/iclr-2027-sets-20-paper-cap-and-limits-first-time-authors)
- **2026-09-02, ICLR 2027 submission policies**:
  - Each author may appear on at most 20 submissions.
  - Each author may have at most one paper in which no author is a qualified reciprocal reviewer, which limits new authors.
  - Over-limit papers may be randomly rejected.
  - About 20% of ICLR 2026 submissions had no reciprocal reviewer; a quarter of those were desk-rejected, and those reviewed were accepted at about half the usual rate.
  - Fewer than 0.2% of authors submitted more than 20 papers.
  - [P] — [ICLR blog](https://blog.iclr.cc/?p=1150); [ICLR 2027 guidelines](https://www.iclr.cc/Conferences/2027/AuthorGuidelines)
  - *Nature Index* reports ICLR 2027 submissions "set to exceed 62,000", citing a Reviewer3 estimate (vendor projection, not an official count). [N] — [Nature Index](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-03153-5)
- **2026-09-10, Google's Paper Assistant Tool (PAT)**: The Gemini-based tool gives private pre-submission feedback to ICLR 2027 authors (11–18 Sep 2026) and is not used in review.
  - At ICML 2026, 35.4% of responding authors with theory results said it found "significant theory gaps", and 31% of those with experiments said it prompted new experiments.
  - [P] — [ICLR blog Sep 2026](https://blog.iclr.cc/2026/09/)
- **ICML 2026 (abstract deadline 23 Jan 2026; full paper 28 Jan 2026)**:
  - Authors with 4 or more submissions must review.
  - LLMs cannot be authors; prompt injection leads to desk rejection.
  - Reviewers chose between Policy A (no LLM use) and Policy B (limited LLM use).
  - [P] — [ICML 2026 CfP](https://icml.cc/Conferences/2026/CallForPapers)
  - **2026-03-18**: Hidden "watermark" prompts in PDFs flagged 795 reviews (about 1%) from 506 Policy-A reviewers. 51 reviewers were removed, and 497 papers (about 2% of submissions) were desk-rejected, linked to 398 reciprocal reviewers. **Series:** ICML 2026 desk-rejection counts. [P] — [ICML blog](https://blog.icml.cc/?p=1167)
- **AAAI-26** (submission deadlines not verified here):
  - The program chairs reported 30,948 submissions, versus about 15,000 for AAAI-25. [S] — [OpenReview post](https://openreview.net/forum/bok|openreview_hosts_recordbreaking_aaai_2026_conference_with_pioneering_ai_review_system)

  - AISTATS 2027: 12 submissions per author, and 3 for authors without an accepted paper at a major AI conference.

### Inferences
- **arXiv cs series** have at least four non-model policy breaks in 12 months: 31 Oct 2025, 21 Jan 2026, 11 Feb 2026 and 1 Oct 2026. The Oct 2025 rule specifically removes review and position papers, the content type most likely to be LLM-generated. Post-Oct-2025 cs counts are therefore censored on the AI-sensitive margin. DiD designs could use cs versus non-cs categories (rule applied only to cs) around 31 Oct 2025, and high- versus low-volume submitters around 1 Oct 2026.
- **Conference submission counts** (ICLR 2025→2026: +68%; AAAI-25→26: about 15k→31k) are shaped by:
  - per-author caps (CVPR from 2025; ICLR, AISTATS and ACL from 2026–27);
  - reciprocal-review obligations;
  - desk-rejection waves for LLM misuse (ICML 2026: 497 papers);
  - data shocks (ICLR 2026 leak).
  These should enter models as dated interventions. Year-on-year growth alone cannot be attributed to LLMs.
- AI-review deployments (ICLR 2025 random assignment; AAAI-26 universal) can be used in their own right as treatments for review-text series. The ICLR 2025 agent was randomised, so it is a clean experiment.

## 3. Funder policy events (AI-specific rules and application-volume controls)

### Takeaway
2023 funder policies (NIH Jun 2023, ARC Jul 2023, DFG Sep 2023, ERC Dec 2023, NSF Dec 2023, China MOST Dec 2023) mostly banned generative AI in **peer review** on confidentiality grounds while allowing applicant use with disclosure. These should not change application volumes much. Volume-relevant interventions came later:
- NIH's cap of 6 applications per PI per calendar year, effective for the 25 Sep 2025 receipt date;
- ERC's extended resubmission restrictions for 2027 calls (letter of 16 Apr 2026);
- NSFC's 2024 rule change allowing resubmission after two unsuccessful years, which raised volumes.

In 2026 several funders relaxed reviewer rules to allow language polishing: ERC (24 Mar 2026), ARC and NHMRC (28 Apr 2026) and UKRI (assessors' clause). Separately, RoRI data show 2022→2025 application growth across 12 funders, averaging 57% (unweighted).

## 4. Confounders: non-AI shocks to publication and application volumes (dated)

### Takeaway
At least five families of non-AI shocks overlap the AI period:
1. The COVID-19 submission surge (Feb–May 2020).
2. Research-evaluation reforms, chiefly China's Feb 2020 "anti-SCI-supremacy" notice.
3. Integrity clean-ups and indexing shocks: Hindawi (Sep 2022 to 2024) and Web of Science delistings (Mar 2023).
4. Open-access mandates: Plan S (2021), the OSTP memo (2022→2025) and NIH public access (1 Jul 2025).
5. US federal disruption in 2025–26: indirect-cost cap attempt, grant terminations, GRFP swings, the 43-day shutdown, NIH funding-model changes and the H-1B fee.

The EU's "Choose Europe" (May 2025) pulls in the opposite direction for EU applications.

### Inferences
- **NIH application series**, Feb 2025 – Feb 2026, have at least six overlapping shocks:
  - terminations;
  - the indirect-cost attempt;
  - the Sep 2025 cap of 6 per PI;
  - the shutdown with rescheduled deadlines;
  - the Jan 2026 removal of paylines;
  - the shift to multiyear funding.
  Monthly ITS on NIH counts around ChatGPT-era dates is unlikely to identify AI. A better design is DiD comparing PIs above and below 6 applications per year, or comparing activity codes exempt from the cap (T, R13) with non-exempt ones.
- **WoS- or Scopus-based publication counts** for 2023 are depressed for MDPI and Hindawi by delistings and retractions. AI analyses should use source-level fixed effects or exclude delisted titles.
- **COVID-19** raises the 2020–21 baseline, so pre-trends estimated from 2019–2022 can understate post-2022 growth.

## 5. Surveys of researchers' AI use (catalog)

### Takeaway
Self-reported use rose steeply, but survey levels are not comparable because of different questions, samples and denominators:
- Wiley: AI for research and publication tasks rose from 45% (2024) to 62% (2025); any work use rose from 57% to 84%.
- Elsevier: AI use at work rose from 37% (2024) to 58% (2025).
- Writing-specific use is lower:
  - Nature 2023: 16.9% of all respondents used generative AI to help write manuscripts and 10.1% for grant applications.
  - Nature 2025: 28.7% used AI to edit their papers and 8.2% to write first drafts.
  - Elsevier 2025: 41% for drafting grant proposals and 38% for drafting papers.
- Peer-review use ranges from about 4–8% (Nature 2025 scenarios) through 32% (IOP 2025) to 53% (Frontiers 2025).

### Inferences
- Adoption roughly doubled between 2023–24 and 2025–26 by the publishers' own trackers (Elsevier 37%→58%; Wiley 45%→62%). This supports treating 2024–2025 as a period of continued diffusion rather than a one-off step in Dec 2022, which favours slope-change (dose-response) specifications over a single level shift.
- Disclosure is weak: in Nature 2025, 18.4% edited with AI without disclosure versus 10.3% with disclosure. Series based on disclosed AI use will understate use. Detector-based series are a needed complement.
- Grant-writing use (Nature 2023: 10–15%; Elsevier 2025: 41% for "drafting grant proposals") is at least as common as manuscript use. AI is therefore a plausible driver of funder volume growth, but RoRI's funder-specific confounders must be modelled.
