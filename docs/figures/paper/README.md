# Publication figures (essay versions)

Figures 1, 4 and 5 without titles (captions go in the text), with an Okabe-Ito colour-blind-safe palette, at
single-column (85 mm) and double-column (175 mm) widths, as SVG, PDF (editable text) and 300-dpi PNG.
Generated by `scripts/plot_paper.py`. Markers: ChatGPT, arXiv rule changes and Claude Opus 5 only. The May 2026
arXiv ban on unchecked LLM output is not marked, because it has no primary source (press reports only). Data
retrieved 15 October 2026.

## Figure 1 — `fig1_index_by_field_{85,175}mm`

**Caption.** New preprints by field, Q1 2020 – Q3 2026, indexed to each field's 2022 quarterly average (= 100).
Dashed lines mark the release of ChatGPT (30 Nov 2022), arXiv's restriction on computer-science review and
position papers (31 Oct 2025), the release of Claude Opus 5 (24 Jul 2026) and arXiv's cap of two submissions per
month (1 Oct 2026).

**Methods.**
- *What is counted:* new preprints per quarter, first versions only.
- *Physics, mathematics and computer science:* new arXiv submissions by primary archive, from arXiv's per-archive yearly statistics, excluding replacements and cross-lists. Physics covers astro-ph, cond-mat, gr-qc, hep-\*, math-ph, nlin, nucl-\*, physics and quant-ph.
- *Biology:* bioRxiv monthly new papers.
- *Medicine:* medRxiv new papers, minus those whose title or abstract mentions COVID-19, SARS-CoV-2 or 2019-nCoV. These were 66% of the 2020 papers and 5% of the 2026 papers.
- *Social sciences:* first versions on PsyArXiv, SocArXiv and EdArXiv (OSF API), plus arXiv econ and q-fin. SSRN and RePEc are not included.

**Caveats.**
- A preprint is not a publication.
- Preprint adoption differs by field, so compare trends within a field, not levels across fields.
- Medicine's 2022 base is low because COVID-19 research displaced other work.
- Aliased arXiv categories put archive sums about 5% above arXiv's official total.
- The social-sciences sample is small.

## Figure 4 — `fig4_math_monthly_{85,175}mm`

**Caption.** New arXiv mathematics papers per month, January 2025 – September 2026. Dashed lines mark arXiv's
tighter endorsement rule for new mathematics authors (10 Dec 2025), the release of Claude Opus 5 (24 Jul 2026)
and arXiv's cap of two submissions per month (1 Oct 2026).

**Methods.**
- *Corpus:* papers whose primary category is math.\*, from arXiv OAI-PMH metadata harvested on 15 Oct 2026 (every record modified since 1 Jan 2025).
- *Dating:* each paper is dated by the month of its first version, read from its identifier (YYMM.NNNNN). The OAI `created` field is not always the first-version date.
- *Comparison with arXiv's per-archive statistics:* these counts are 6–13% lower, because those statistics also count aliased categories such as math-ph/math.MP. The trend agrees: Q3 2026 vs Q3 2025 is +80% here and +78% in the per-archive series.
- *What the counts measure:* moderated, announced papers, not submissions.
- *Timing:* the cap was announced and applied on the same day, so the September peak cannot be a rush ahead of it.
- October 2026 is incomplete and not shown.

## Figure 5 — `fig5_math_profile_{85,175}mm`

**Caption.** New arXiv mathematics papers, January 2025 – September 2026, by month.
- **a,** share of papers with at least one author who posted five or more mathematics papers in the same quarter. Orange: all author names. Grey: full first names only, with the 1% most frequent surnames removed.
- **b,** single-author share.
- **c,** share mentioning an AI tool or model in the title, abstract or comments.
- **d,** number of papers mentioning Erdős.

Dashed lines as in Fig. 4.

**Methods.**
- *Corpus:* the same as Fig. 4.
- *Author matching:* authors are matched by normalised name strings, because arXiv metadata carry no author identifiers.
- *Namesakes:* they inflate the level of panel a. Keeping full first names only barely changes it (Q3 2025 → Q3 2026: 5.2% → 18.0%). Also removing the 1% most frequent surnames, which hold 36% of author slots, lowers it to 1.8% → 5.9%, but the roughly threefold rise holds.
- *Team size and timing:* the rise appears in every team size (single-author papers: 2.8% → 12.1%) and begins in March 2026.
- *AI mentions:* matched with keywords in metadata only, not full text, so they undercount AI use.
- *Partial October:* October 2026 is partial and opens a new quarter, so its prolific share (2.9%) falls mechanically. It is excluded.

## Figure 8 — `fig8_within_field_{85,175}mm`

**Caption.** New preprints in computational and wet-lab biology (**a**, bioRxiv) and in theoretical and experimental
physics (**b**, arXiv), Q1 2020 – Q3 2026, indexed to each group's 2022 quarterly average (= 100). Dashed lines as in
Fig. 1; the arXiv rule markers apply to panel b only.

**Methods.**
- *Biology (a):* bioRxiv version-1 preprints by subject category, from the bioRxiv API's monthly per-category totals
  (`count_new_papers` of the details endpoint filtered by category).
  - Their sum matches bioRxiv's official monthly new-paper totals exactly from July 2019. January–June 2019 is
    about 4% lower, because epidemiology and clinical-trials preprints, which moved to medRxiv, cannot be queried by
    category.
  - Groups, fixed before looking at growth (as proposed by the essay session):
    - computational: bioinformatics, systems biology, genomics;
    - wet lab: biochemistry, cell, developmental, molecular, plant and cancer biology, microbiology, immunology,
      physiology, pharmacology and toxicology, pathology, synthetic biology;
    - mixed: the other ten categories (neuroscience is the largest).
  - COVID-19 preprints are included, because the per-category totals carry no topic flag.
    - In a full read of May–June 2022 they were 5.1% of computational, 5.9% of wet-lab and 2.1% of mixed preprints
      (`data/raw/biorxiv_covid_sample_2022.csv`).
    - Including them therefore inflates the 2022 base of the two contrasted groups by about the same amount.
- *Physics (b):* new arXiv submissions by primary archive, from arXiv's yearly statistics pages.
  - Theory: hep-th, hep-ph, gr-qc, nucl-th.
  - Experiment: hep-ex, nucl-ex.
- Data retrieved 16 October 2026. Scripts: `harvest_rxiv_categories.py`, `analyze_within_field.py`,
  `plot_within_field.py`.

**Numbers for the text** (growth in January–September against the same months a year earlier):

| Group | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | 2026 | Index Q3 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| bioRxiv computational | +7% | +9% | +20% | +18% | 164 |
| bioRxiv wet lab | +9% | +12% | +13% | +8% | 152 |
| bioRxiv mixed | +9% | +11% | +12% | +13% | 159 |
| arXiv theory | 0% | +6% | +6% | +20% | 149 |
| arXiv experiment | −7% | +7% | +9% | +11% | 119 |

- **Biology.**
  - Computational preprints outgrew wet-lab ones only from 2025, by 7–10 points a year; in 2023–2024 they grew slightly slower.
  - The gap rests mostly on bioinformatics:
    - bioinformatics: +22% in 2025 and +27% in 2026, and 63% of the computational group in 2026;
    - genomics: +15% and +7%;
    - systems biology: +21% and +5%.
  - Much of bioinformatics is itself AI research (protein and genomic models). The contrast therefore mixes AI as a topic with AI as a tool, the same caveat as for computer science.
- **Physics.**
  - Theory grows 20% in 2026, far outside its 2017–2025 range (−6% to +6%).
  - Experiment grows 11%, inside its own range (−13% to +22%).
  - The experimental series is small (about 430 papers a quarter in 2026) and volatile.
  - By pair: hep-th +24% vs hep-ex +14%; nucl-th +16% vs nucl-ex +2%.
- **Base-year check.**
  - Against January–September 2019 instead of 2022, theory is +46% and experiment +34%.
  - In biology, against 2019 wet lab is +118% and computational +86%, because of the 2020 surge in wet-lab preprints (COVID-19 and late adoption).
  - The biology contrast is therefore a 2025–2026 divergence, not a long-run difference.
- **arXiv q-bio,** for comparison with bioRxiv's computational categories: +19% (2023), +19% (2024), +16% (2025), −7% (2026).

**medRxiv (no figure in the essay set; static chart `../fig9_medrxiv_categories.png`).**
- *Overall:* excluding COVID-19, medRxiv grew 27% in 2024, 21% in 2025 and 22% in 2026, so there is no acceleration in 2026.
- *Fastest-growing subjects in 2026:*
  - health informatics +54%;
  - health systems and quality improvement +42%;
  - epidemiology +32%;
  - neurology and radiology +27%;
  - oncology +22%.
- *Flat subjects:* public and global health +3%, genetic and genomic medicine +3%, infectious diseases +1%.
- *Public datasets:* preprints naming one in title or abstract (NHANES, UK Biobank, Global Burden of Disease, Mendelian randomisation, FAERS, MIMIC, SEER, BRFSS):
  - 405 in January–September 2022 (5.0% of new preprints) and 915 in January–September 2026 (6.9%);
  - that is ×2.3, against ×1.6 for all new preprints;
  - shares in 2026 are highest in intensive care (24%, MIMIC), genetic and genomic medicine (23%), cardiovascular medicine (14%), epidemiology (13%) and nutrition (12%).
