# Journals and outlets for an English-language position paper on AI and the academic system

*Pages were accessed on 2026-10-15 unless a finding says otherwise. "Not verified" means the page could not be retrieved, or it was retrieved but did not state the detail. Taylor & Francis (tandfonline.com), Wiley Online Library, MIT Press Direct, Cell Press, Royal Society Publishing and science.org all returned HTTP 403 to automated access. Details for those venues come from search-engine result summaries and are labelled "(search summary)". The CSIC journal platform (redc.revistas.csic.es) failed with a TLS error and then HTTP 503. AI-policy classes follow the coordinator's scheme: (a) allows AI-generated text with disclosure; (b) restricts it, for example to language editing only or by requiring editor permission; (c) prohibits AI-generated text.*

## 1. Which venues fit the paper, in which article type and at what length, and do they take unsolicited submissions?

### Takeaway
Six journals take an unsolicited argumentative paper of about 6,000–8,000 words as a refereed article: Minerva, Postdigital Science and Education, Research Evaluation, Science and Public Policy, Interdisciplinary Science Reviews and Humanities & Social Sciences Communications. Research Evaluation and Science and Public Policy also have a discussion-paper or "Perspectives" route. The 1,500–4,000-word condensed versions fit the HSSC Comment (≤4,000 words, refereed), the PLOS Biology Essay (~3,500–4,000 words, refereed, no fee), the Nature Human Behaviour Perspective (3,000–4,000) or Comment (≤1,800), the EMBO Reports Science & Society article (≤3,500, no APC) and the Big Data & Society Commentary (≤3,000). Nature's Comment and World View sections take no unsolicited pieces. Synthese, eLife and Quantitative Science Studies fit poorly.

### Cited Findings

**Science policy and research evaluation**
- **Minerva** (Springer, hybrid). The journal "is devoted to the study of ideas, traditions, cultures and institutions in science, higher education and research ... It features articles, essay reviews and 'special' issues on themes of topical importance ... Its contributions are peer-reviewed." — [Minerva aims & scope](https://link.springer.com/journal/11024/aims-and-scope)
- Minerva gives no separate essay type. Its length rule is: "The general length of our papers are 8000-10,000 words (including references, footnotes, tables and figures)." Abstract 150–250 words. Standard online submission. — [Minerva submission guidelines](https://link.springer.com/journal/11024/submission-guidelines)
- **Science and Public Policy** (OUP, hybrid). It "invites submission for Articles, Perspective Papers and Special Sections." Articles are "up to 8,000 - 10,000 words, including: title, abstract, tables, graphs and references", with an abstract of ≤150 words. — [SPP author guidelines](https://academic.oup.com/spp/pages/author-guidelines)
- SPP Perspective Papers "should provide opinions on timely policy-related issues". Their titles "must begin with 'Perspectives:'" and they "should not exceed five pages". They "will be reviewed by the editors and thus will contain a footnote explaining that perspectives papers are not reviewed by independent referees". Letters to the Editor run to 1,000 words. — [SPP author guidelines](https://academic.oup.com/spp/pages/author-guidelines)
- **Research Evaluation** (OUP, hybrid). "Articles can be up to 8,000 - 10,000 words and should aim to address an evaluation problem". "Voices from the Field" pieces run to 1,200–1,500 words. They are aimed at practitioners, and authors submit a preliminary abstract and outline for editor feedback. — [Research Evaluation author guidelines](https://academic.oup.com/rev/pages/author-guidelines)
- Research Evaluation: "Discussion papers dedicated to the development of an argument on a specific controversial topic are generally published by invitation only. We may also accept uninvited discussion papers, provided they are related to key topics that fit well with the journal's objectives and scope ... At the Editors' discretion, discussion papers may be peer-reviewed or considered without external peer review." No word limit is given for discussion papers. The same page also says "All article types undergo the Journal's standard peer review process", which conflicts with the discussion-paper wording. — [Research Evaluation author guidelines](https://academic.oup.com/rev/pages/author-guidelines)
- **Scientometrics** (Springer, hybrid). The submission guidelines list no essay, perspective or commentary type and give no length limit. — [Scientometrics submission guidelines](https://link.springer.com/journal/11192/submission-guidelines)
- **Quantitative Science Studies** (MIT Press, gold OA). Article types were not verified, because MIT Press Direct returns 403. According to a search summary of the ISSI page, the journal publishes "theoretical and empirical research on science and the scientific workforce". — [ISSI QSS page (search summary)](https://issidev.cwts.nl/publications/quantitative-science-studies/)

Comments "are usually commissioned but may also be submitted". - **Patterns** (Cell Press). A Perspective is typically about 2,000–4,000 words, with 2–5 figures, 20–60 references and a summary of ≤150 words. ### Inferences
- The 6,000–8,000-word full paper fits the general-article length of Minerva (8,000–10,000 including references), SPP and Research Evaluation (8,000–10,000), PSE (~7,000), ISR (4,000–8,000 including references, so it may need trimming), HSSC (~8,000 excluding references) and Higher Education (≤8,000 including references). Only Research Evaluation (discussion paper), SPP (Perspectives) and AI & Society (Open Forum) name an argumentative format explicitly.
- The condensed versions fall naturally into four bands. The 3,000–4,000-word band matches the HSSC Comment, PLOS Biology Essay, NHB Perspective and EMBO Science & Society. The 2,500–3,000 band matches the ISR Commentary and BDS Commentary. The 1,000–2,000 band matches the PSE Comment, NHB Comment, AI & Society Curmudgeon and PLOS Perspective. The 1,200–1,500 band matches the Research Evaluation "Voices from the field".
- Synthese (no response papers or surveys; technical philosophy), eLife (life-science features ≤2,000 words), QSS (empirical scientometrics) and Philosophy & Technology commentaries (invitation-only) are poor fits for this paper.

### Gaps
- No word limits were verified for Accountability in Research, Studies in Higher Education, Learning, Media and Technology, Social Epistemology or Learned Publishing, all because of 403 blocks. The Patterns limits rest only on a search summary.
- The research-article length for Philosophy & Technology was not found. No limit was stated for Research Evaluation discussion papers or the RIPR Comment.
- REDC: nothing on article types was verified.

## 2. What does each venue's AI policy allow? (classification for an openly disclosed, agent-assisted draft)

### Takeaway
Most peer-reviewed candidates are class (a). They allow AI-generated or AI-drafted text if it is disclosed and the author keeps accountability. This covers all Springer Nature titles (including BMC, Nature Portfolio and, presumably, EMBO Reports), OUP, PLOS, eLife, Elsevier (Cell Press), Wiley and SAGE, as well as Taylor & Francis at publisher level. Elsevier's current policy explicitly names "AI agents and deep research tools", which is the closest match to the author's workflow. AI & Society is (b): it "strongly discourage[s]" LLM use beyond grammar and translation. Issues in Science and Technology and Times Higher Education are (c). SocArXiv is (c) for verbatim AI-generated text. No peer-reviewed journal was found that explicitly invites AI-co-written essays. Postdigital Science and Education, EPI and RIPR have published reflexive or AI-assisted, disclosed work.

### Cited Findings

**Publisher-level policies (quoted)**
- **Springer Nature / Nature Portfolio / BMC (current risk framework)**: this is class (a).
  - Its "Amber" tier covers "Drafting explanatory summaries", "Comparing results to existing literature" and "Extensive copy editing or writing support". This tier is "Permitted with human oversight, verification, and transparency through disclosure."
  - Not permitted ("Red"): "Generating hypotheses, analyses or conclusions and presenting them as human-derived", "Using an LLM to generate core research reasoning without disclosure" and "Assigning authorship or accountability to AI systems or tools".
  - Source: [Nature Portfolio AI policy](https://www.nature.com/nature-portfolio/editorial-policies/ai). BMC uses the same framework: [BMC editorial policies](https://link.springer.com/brands/bmc/editorial-policies).
- **Springer journal pages (older text, still on Minerva, Scientometrics, Higher Education, PSE, AI & Society, Synthese)**: this is class (a).
  - "Use of an LLM should be properly documented in the Methods section (and if a Methods section is not available, in a suitable alternative part) of the manuscript."
  - "AI assisted copy editing" need not be declared, but this exemption covers "wording and formatting changes" and does "not include generative editorial work and autonomous content creation. In all cases, there must be human accountability for the final version of the text".
  - Source: [Minerva submission guidelines](https://link.springer.com/journal/11024/submission-guidelines) (identical text on the other Springer pages listed).
- **OUP (Science and Public Policy, Research Evaluation)**: this is class (a).
  - "The use of AI (for example, to help generate content or images, write code, process data, or for translation) should be disclosed both in cover letters to editors and in the Methods or Acknowledgements section of manuscripts." — [SPP author guidelines](https://academic.oup.com/spp/pages/author-guidelines); [Research Evaluation author guidelines](https://academic.oup.com/rev/pages/author-guidelines)
  - OUP's general page uses "must be disclosed". — [OUP ethics](https://academic.oup.com/pages/for-authors/journals/preparing-and-submitting-your-manuscript/ethics)
- **SAGE (Big Data & Society, Social Studies of Science, ST&HV, ISR)**: this is class (a), with a caveat.
  - SAGE distinguishes "assistive (and no longer requiring disclosure), generative (requiring disclosure), and prohibitive" uses.
  - "The primary or partial use of AI tools and/or LLMs that produce content such as references, text, images ... must be disclosed upon submission so the editorial team can evaluate the content generated."
  - "submissions will not be rejected solely because of the disclosed use of GenAI tools".
  - Caveat: "Inappropriate use of GenAI includes ... Generation of data or submission using a series of prompts".
  - Source: [SAGE AI policy](https://www.sagepub.com/journals/publication-ethics-policies/artificial-intelligence-policy)
- **Taylor & Francis (Accountability in Research, Studies in Higher Education, Learning, Media and Technology, Social Epistemology)**: class (a) at publisher level; journal-level wording not verified.
  - "Authors must clearly acknowledge within the article or book any use of Generative AI tools through a statement that includes: the full name of the tool used (with version number), how it was used and the reason for use."
  - Not acceptable: "Text or code generation without rigorous revision".
  - Warning: "some journals may not allow use of Generative AI tools beyond language improvement, therefore authors are advised to review the 'Instructions for authors'".
  - Source: [T&F AI policy](https://taylorandfrancis.com/our-policies/ai-policy/)
- **Wiley (Learned Publishing)**: this is class (a).
  - "Authors may only use AI Technology as an additional tool in their writing process, not a replacement for their own expertise and judgement".
  - "Authors should document all AI Technology used, including its purpose, whether it influenced key arguments or conclusions, and how they personally reviewed and verified any AI-generated content. Authors must also disclose their use of AI Technologies when submitting".
  - Source: [Wiley best-practice guidelines](https://authors.wiley.com/ethics-guidelines/index.html)
- **Elsevier / Cell Press (Patterns)**: this is class (a).
  - "these tools, including AI agents and deep research tools, are helping researchers to synthesize complex literature ... Authors preparing a manuscript for an Elsevier journal can use AI tools to support them in these tasks. However, these tools must never be used as a substitute for human critical thinking, expertise and evaluation."
  - Disclosure goes "in a separate AI declaration statement", headed "Declaration of generative AI and AI-assisted technologies in the manuscript preparation process".
  - Source: [Elsevier generative AI policy](https://www.elsevier.com/about/policies-and-standards/generative-ai-policies-for-journals)
  - The Cell Press-specific page was not checked (403).
- **PLOS (PLOS Biology)**: this is class (a).
  - "AI tools may be used to support development or revision of a PLOS submission. However, articles should report the listed authors' own work and ideas".
  - Disclosures must name the tools, say which aspects were affected and give "a description of how the user evaluated the validity of the tool's outputs". They belong in Methods, Acknowledgements or figure legends.
  - Source: [PLOS ethical publishing practice](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/s/ethical-publishing-practice)
- **eLife**: this is class (a). LLM tools "cannot be listed as or serve as co-authors of a paper, and their use must be clearly described in the Materials and Methods section." — [eLife author instructions](https://elife-rp.msubmit.net/html/elife-rp_author_instructions.html)
- **MIT Press (QSS)**: class (a) per a secondary source only. A third-party policy checker dated May 2026 summarises MIT Press as permitting "AI tools to produce text ... provided authors inform their editors and are transparent". It also notes that individual titles may be stricter. Not verified on MIT Press. — [BioRender policy checker, MIT Press/Daedalus (search summary)](https://policycheck.tools.biorender.com/journal/daedalus)
- **AAAS (Science Policy Forum)**: class (a) per secondary sources only.
  - Secondary sources say Science moved from a January 2023 ban to a disclosure model about ten months later. Under that model, AI use is disclosed in the cover letter, the acknowledgments and the methods.
  - A Kennesaw State page still describes a ban and looks out of date.
  - science.org returned 403, so this is not verified. — [Manusights, Science AI policy (search summary)](https://manusights.com/blog/science-ai-policy)

**Journal-specific restrictions or prohibitions**
- **AI & Society**: this is class (b). "LLM use for tasks other than grammar and translation, and high similarity submissions (archived/published) are strongly discouraged. Pre-publications should be acknowledged in the declaration section." — [AI & Society submission guidelines](https://link.springer.com/journal/146/submission-guidelines)
- **Issues in Science and Technology**: this is class (c).
  - "Do not use generative AI to write any part of your pitch or article. We want your great ideas, not the superficially polished prose and questionable sourcing of an LLM." — [Issues contact/submissions](https://issues.org/contact-us/)
  - "Those who write for us agree to refrain from using generative AI to write copy and to disclose any use of AI." — [Issues AI policy](https://issues.org/ai-policy/)
- **Times Higher Education**: this is class (c). "THE does not encourage the use of AI in article preparation and does not accept any article that is significantly written by AI and/or conveys a false impression of the author's own experience, expertise or opinion." Any AI use must be declared and explained. — [THE AI policy](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/times-higher-educations-policy-ai-use-article-preparation)
- **LSE Impact Blog**: this is class (a), with a limit. "We do not accept wholly AI-generated content. AI used in the creation process must be declared." — [LSE Impact Blog submissions](https://blogs.lse.ac.uk/impactofsocialsciences/submissions/)
- **Nature Correspondence**: this is class (a). "Use of an LLM should instead be properly documented in a footnote to the main text." — [Nature: other submissions](https://www.nature.com/nature/for-authors/other-subs)
- **EPI**: no general AI policy was found. One restrictive signal: "En ningún caso se aceptan traducciones hechas con traductores automáticos." Where the author supplies the English version, a translation-agency certificate is required. — [EPI submissions](https://revista.profesionaldelainformacion.com/index.php/EPI/about/submissions)
- **SocArXiv** (AI policy dated 8 Mar 2026, updated 28 Sep 2026): this is class (c) for verbatim AI text.
  - "These are unacceptable uses: Generating text which is used verbatim (including whole paragraphs and sections)".
  - Literature searches are acceptable "when the paper also reports on original research and the author attests to thorough human supervision".
  - It disallows "Superficial, high-level theory synthesis" papers.
  - "Exceptions ... may be made for work that has been peer-reviewed".
  - "Failure to disclose, or implausible disclosures, are grounds for rejection."
  - Source: [SocArXiv AI policy](https://socopen.org/ai-policy/)
- **arXiv**: this is class (a). arXiv requires authors "to report in their work any significant use of sophisticated tools ... we now include in particular text-to-text generative AI among those that should be reported". Authors "take full responsibility for all its contents, irrespective of how the contents were generated." — [arXiv moderation](https://info.arxiv.org/help/moderation/index.html)

### Inferences
- The author's disclosure (AI agents did the literature search, verification and first drafting; the author set the thesis, structure and judgements and did the final revision) maps onto Springer Nature's "Amber" tier. It is permitted with disclosure. Hiding it would be "Red". In Springer Nature venues open disclosure is therefore required, not merely tolerated.
- In SAGE journals the statement must make the human authorship of thesis, structure and judgements explicit. Otherwise an editor could read "agents drafted the paper" as "Generation of ... submission using a series of prompts".
- For Wiley venues the statement must say "whether it influenced key arguments or conclusions". For PLOS it must say how outputs were validated. For OUP it must also appear in the cover letter. For T&F it must give tool names and versions.
- Postdigital Science and Education is the most demonstrably receptive peer-reviewed venue for a paper that treats its own AI-assisted making as evidence.

## 6. Short-format, high-visibility outlets

### Takeaway
Nature's Comment and World View sections are commission-only. Its Correspondence (<200 words) is open and allows LLM use if documented in a footnote. Times Higher Education and Issues in Science and Technology reject substantially AI-written text, so any piece for them must be written by the author personally. The LSE Impact Blog (800–1,200 words) accepts declared AI-assisted writing that is not wholly AI-generated. The Conversation takes academics only, but its AI policy was not found. Science's Policy Forum rules could not be verified.

### Cited Findings
- **Nature**: "Unsolicited contributions are not accepted. Comments, Book Reviews and World Views are part of a commission-only section". Correspondence "submissions must be less than 200 words in length, with no more than 2 references", sent in the body of an email. "If you have not heard from us after 3 weeks, please presume that we cannot publish your submission." — [Nature: other submissions](https://www.nature.com/nature/for-authors/other-subs)
- **Nature Human Behaviour**: Comment ≤1,800 words, with peer review at the editors' discretion. World Views are "by prior arrangement only ... Unsolicited contributions will not normally be considered, although prospective authors are welcome to make proposals." — [NHB content types](https://www.nature.com/nathumbehav/content)
- **Times Higher Education**:
  - Opinion articles are "around 750 - 1000 words long". Features are "usually 1,500-2,500 words".
  - "please email a short summary of your proposal ... We will also consider completed articles".
  - "it may be several weeks before we are able to give a verdict".
  - Source: [Writing for THE](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/writing-times-higher-education)
  - AI policy as quoted in §2. — [THE AI policy](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/times-higher-educations-policy-ai-use-article-preparation)
- **LSE Impact Blog**:

- **The Conversation**: "We only work with academics or researchers at universities and qualifying research institutes within their field of professional expertise ... ## 7. Preprint options, including arXiv's October 2025 computer-science policy

### Takeaway
arXiv CS (cs.CY, cs.DL) will now consider a position paper only after it has passed peer review at a journal or conference, with the journal reference or DOI supplied. Position papers are not an accepted content type anywhere on arXiv. physics.soc-ph accepts research papers, not essays. SocArXiv accepts essays, but its 2026 AI policy rejects verbatim AI-generated paragraphs; it may make exceptions for peer-reviewed work. MetaArXiv is active and on-topic, though its AI policy was not found. Zenodo accepts anything and mints a DOI, but offers little curation or visibility.

### Cited Findings
- **arXiv, 31 Oct 2025**:
  - "Before being considered for submission to arXiv's CS category, review articles and position papers must now be accepted at a journal or a conference and complete successful peer review." Authors "must include documentation of successful peer review".
  - The change follows an "unmanageable influx of review articles and position papers".
  - "the review conducted at conference workshops generally does not meet the same standard of rigor".
  - Policy-wise: "review articles and position papers are not (and have never been) listed as part of the accepted content types".
  - On scientific papers about science and society: "Yes, arXiv has always released these types of scientific papers, for example in cs.CY or physics.soc-ph. These are scientific research papers and are not subject to this moderation practice change."
  - Source: [arXiv blog, 31 Oct 2025](https://blog.arxiv.org/2025/10/31/attention-authors-updated-practice-for-review-articles-and-position-papers-in-arxiv-cs-category/)
- **arXiv category scope**:
  - cs.CY "Covers impact of computers on society, computer ethics, information technology and public policy". cs.DL covers digital libraries and document creation.
  - physics.soc-ph covers the "Structure, dynamics and collective behavior of societies and groups ... Quantitative analysis of social networks".
  - Source: [arXiv category taxonomy](https://arxiv.org/category_taxonomy)
- **arXiv moderation**:
  - "Submissions that do not contain original or substantive research, including course projects, research proposals, news, or information about political causes ... may be declined."
  - "Each author may submit up to two new submissions per calendar month with a limit of three active submissions."
  - Source: [arXiv moderation](https://info.arxiv.org/help/moderation/index.html)
  - arXiv's content-type page lists research articles as the primary type. — [arXiv content types](https://info.arxiv.org/help/policies/content-types.html)
- **SocArXiv**:
  - It accepts "original research, reviews, essays, critiques, pedagogical papers, and comments on other work" in "Social and Behavioral Sciences, Education, and Law".
  - Arts and Humanities were accepted only "Prior to September 2025".
  - It requires "a publicly viewable ORCID profile" and expects "to post papers submitted for moderation within 3 days".
  - Source: [SocArXiv moderation policy (revised Oct 2025)](https://socopen.org/moderation-policy/)
  - AI policy as quoted in §2. — [SocArXiv AI policy](https://socopen.org/ai-policy/)
- **MetaArXiv**:
  - The OSF provider record describes "An interdisciplinary archive of articles focused on improving research transparency and reproducibility", maintained by BITSS, with "allow_submissions: True" and "reviews_workflow: pre-moderation". — [OSF API, MetaArXiv](https://api.osf.io/v2/providers/preprints/metaarxiv/)
  - Preprints were accepted on 13–15 Oct 2026, and the listing totals 1,059. — [OSF API, MetaArXiv preprints](https://api.osf.io/v2/providers/preprints/metaarxiv/preprints/?page%5Bsize%5D=3&sort=-date_created)
- **Zenodo**: "Any status is accepted, from any stage of the research lifecycle." "All formats are allowed." "For textual items, English is preferred but all languages are accepted." — [Zenodo policies](https://about.zenodo.org/policies/)

### Inferences
- A sensible sequence is to deposit on Zenodo or MetaArXiv at submission, including the AI-use documentation package. Cross-post to SocArXiv and arXiv cs.CY (cross-listed to cs.DL) only after acceptance, citing the DOI.
- AI & Society says "high similarity submissions (archived/published) are strongly discouraged", so a public preprint could count against that journal specifically.

### Gaps
- MetaArXiv's and OSF's AI policies, and whether MetaArXiv accepts non-empirical essays.
- Each target journal's preprint policy, apart from AI & Society, was not checked.
- arXiv moderators' treatment of a peer-reviewed position paper in physics.soc-ph.

## 8. Credibility risks and ranked recommendation

### Takeaway
MDPI and Frontiers titles should be avoided as a matter of credibility. The paper criticises volume-driven, APC-funded mega-journal publishing, and MDPI titles have faced Web of Science delisting. EPI's paid fast track (£2,500, paid reviewers) carries a similar, if milder, tension. The ranked recommendation for the full paper is Minerva, then Postdigital Science and Education, then Research Evaluation (or Science and Public Policy as an alternative). All are class (a), Springer and OUP, and refereed. Condensed spin-offs suit HSSC, PLOS Biology, NHB, EMBO Reports and Big Data & Society.

### Cited Findings
- In March 2023 Clarivate delisted about 50 journals from Web of Science, including two MDPI titles (IJERPH and JRFM) and 19 Hindawi titles. IJERPH had a 2022 impact factor of 4.614 and "published about 17,000 articles that year". — [Chemistry World (search summary)](https://chemistryworld.com/news/sanctioning-of-50-journals-raises-concerns-over-special-issues-in-mega-journals/4017315.article); [InfoDocket summary of Science news (search summary)](https://www.infodocket.com/2023/03/28/report-fast-growing-open-access-journals-stripped-of-coveted-impact-factors/)
- EPI's "Publicación OA Express" costs "GBP 2500" and relies on reviewers "que reciben una compensación". — [EPI OA Express](https://revista.profesionaldelainformacion.com/index.php/EPI/announcement/view/436)
- REDC's 2025 corpus includes an MDPI-focused bibliometric study: Gregorio-Chaviano et al. 2025, "Caracterización y análisis de la producción científica latinoamericana en revistas de la editorial MDPI". — [doi:10.3989/redc.2025.2.1615](https://doi.org/10.3989/redc.2025.2.1615)

### Inferences
- **MDPI and Frontiers**: publishing a critique of metric-driven, high-volume publishing in an MDPI or Frontiers title would invite the charge of performative contradiction from referees and readers. Treat these as a credibility risk and do not submit there. This is the coordinator's requested flag; no Frontiers-specific evidence was gathered.
- **Ranking, full paper (6,000–8,000 words)**:
  1. **Minerva**. Fit: §1, §2 and §4 strongly, with §3 and §5 as policy implications. Refereed (double-blind). JIF 2.9; Scopus Q1. CRUE-covered OA. Class (a). Median first decision 9 days.
  2. **Postdigital Science and Education**. Covers all five sections. Shows proven openness to reflexive human–AI writing. CRUE-covered. Class (a). It needs explicit differentiation from Manabu 2026, and its WoS status should be checked.
  3. **Research Evaluation**, as an Article or uninvited discussion paper. Fit: §1, §2 and §4. SSCI JIF 2.7. Class (a), with disclosure also in the cover letter. The subscription route is free. **Science and Public Policy** is the alternative if §5 (geopolitics of knowledge infrastructure) is foregrounded.
- **Down-ranked**:
  - AI & Society is class (b), and its preprint similarity concern applies.
  - Issues and THE are class (c).
  - SocArXiv is class (c) before peer review.
  - The T&F venues are unverified at journal level.
  - Synthese, eLife, QSS and Scientometrics are poor format fits.
  - HSSC is a strong fit but costs a €1,590 APC that CRUE does not cover. It remains the best home for a 4,000-word Comment version.
- **Submission order**: Minerva, then PSE, then Research Evaluation or SPP. Fast Springer desk decisions make the higher-standing option cheap to try first. Never submit in parallel. Condensed spin-offs should each carry a distinct argument (for example, §5 alone as an HSSC Comment or BDS Commentary, or §3 as a PLOS Biology Essay or ISR Commentary), and the overlap should be disclosed to editors so they are not treated as redundant publication.

### Gaps
- No primary Clarivate or Frontiers source was retrieved. The MDPI delisting facts rest on search summaries of Chemistry World and Science coverage.
- The author's institutional CRUE quota and any T&F or SAGE deal at their university were not checked.
